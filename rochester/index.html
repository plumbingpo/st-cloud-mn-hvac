Two situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: switch the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can flood the drain pan and hurt the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or gives off a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and give us a call. That small step can prevent a much larger repair.

Keeping Your Rochester HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the first heat wave of the year.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but left alone for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.
